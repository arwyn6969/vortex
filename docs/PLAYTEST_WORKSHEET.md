# Five fresh journeys

Status: prepared for use; **no participant sessions recorded yet**. This worksheet tests the local [gameplay increment](GAMEPLAY_PASS_2026-09-12.md). It is not evidence of enjoyment or accessibility until real people use it.

## Setup

Allow about 25 minutes per person. Use a fresh browser profile or a new seeker, keeping other journeys intact. Record the build/commit, device, browser, viewport, input method and whether the person has played before. Use anonymous participant IDs; ask permission before recording audio or video. Do not record personal wallet details.

Say: “This is a short frog adventure. Please play as you normally would and say what you are trying to do. There are no wrong choices.” Avoid explaining Look, rites, stories or the Atlas before the player encounters them. If they need help, give it, but record the time and exact assistance.

## Prompts and observations

| Time / stage | Prompt | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 3 minutes | “Start a journey and do something you think matters.” | First meaningful choice; time; missed controls; whether reading or interacting felt preferable. |
| After the first choice | “What are you trying to do next?” | Their own description; whether the compass helped; confusion between Look, rite and story. |
| First errand | “Help a character and follow the story.” | Can they find the destination, deliver and understand the return step? Note repeated reading or unnecessary crossings. |
| Atlas detour | “Find an idea that interests you, inspect why it connects to another idea, then return to the game.” | Source/interpretation understanding; reading-place recovery; whether following an encounter changes the route clearly. |
| Solar crossing | “Help the passengers cross.” | First attempt; which clue they used; retries; helper/hint/delegation discovery; whether failure feedback makes sense. |
| Return to a changed place | “What, if anything, is different here?” | Spontaneous notice of the prop and its relationship to their actual choice. Do not point out the illustration first. |
| End | “Which moment would you tell someone about? What would you remove? Would you keep playing?” | Verbatim short answers; separate observed behavior from guesses about motivation. |

If a natural journey has not reached the crossing after 15 minutes, use a separate test journey prepared at Boundaries with the solar errand active. Label those observations **scenario-assisted**; exclude them from onboarding results. Offer one group the Mercy friendship and one group the ordinary hint, without explaining the difference beforehand.

For input/accessibility sessions, test the person's usual tools. Include keyboard-only operation, screen-reader ticket names/grouping, capacity updates, error discovery, focus after submission, route changes, zoom/reflow and reduced motion. Simulated accessibility trees do not replace that session.

## Decisions after the sessions

Aim for at least **four of five** newcomers to make a meaningful choice within three minutes and explain the next objective without coaching. Treat this as a small directional sample, not a statistical guarantee. Any lost save, duplicated reward or blocked completion is a release blocker. Repeated confusion by two people deserves a concrete UI/copy change before more content is added.

Compare puzzle satisfaction and recall, not completion speed alone. Delegation is a valid play choice. Keep the puzzle only if it adds an interesting decision or observation for these players. If consequences are missed, improve their timing, contrast or character reaction before multiplying scene illustrations.

For each action item, record: reproduction, expected/observed behavior, frequency, severity, proposed change, owner, and the evidence needed to close it. Keep untested assumptions separate from defects that were reproduced.
